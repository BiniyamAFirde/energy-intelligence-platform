# Exploratory Data Analysis (Phase 6)

Generated by `python -m energy_platform.analytics.report` against the live
database. Every number below is from that run (`data/processed/eda_report.json`,
`reports/eda/*.png`), not from general knowledge about energy consumption --
regenerate the report to reproduce them.

**Dataset**: 3 sites (Moose, Gator, Peacock; all US/Eastern), 60 buildings,
1,052,400 hourly electricity readings, 51,931 hourly weather readings,
2016-01-01 to 2018-01-01.

## Methodology

All analysis runs through the layered architecture (`API/CLI -> analytics
services -> analytics repositories -> PostgreSQL`), not ad-hoc scripts
reading CSVs. Seasonality profiles (hour-of-day, day-of-week, month) convert
`energy_measurements.ts` to each site's local time via Postgres `AT TIME
ZONE` before grouping -- grouping on the stored UTC timestamps directly
would misattribute occupancy patterns to the wrong local hour. Correlations
use Pearson's r with pairwise deletion of missing values (`services/stats.py`),
each reported with its actual sample size.

## 1. How consumption varies over time

The daily portfolio total (`reports/eda/01_overall_consumption.png`) sits in
a fairly stable 275,000-350,000 kWh/day band for the full two years, with a
visible weekly sawtooth and a handful of sharp single-day drops (data
dropouts, e.g. ~200,000 kWh/day around 2016-08 and 2017-10) rather than a
strong seasonal (summer/winter) trend. That absence of a visible seasonal
swing is corroborated independently by the weather correlations in section 6.

## 2. Daily / weekly / monthly patterns

**Hour-of-day** (`02_hourly_profile.png`, local time, averaged across all
60 buildings): a clear occupancy curve. Overnight trough of ~188-192 kWh
(02:00-04:00), rising sharply from 04:00, plateauing at ~250-252 kWh from
10:00-14:00, declining through the evening. Peak-to-trough ratio ≈ 1.33 --
notable that the overnight *floor* is ~75% of the midday peak, implying a
large base load (HVAC, refrigeration, always-on equipment) independent of
occupancy, consistent with a portfolio dominated by large institutional
buildings (see section 4).

**Day-of-week** (`03_day_of_week_profile.png`): weekdays average 228-234
kWh, Saturday/Sunday average 199-203 kWh -- a 14-17% weekday premium. Real,
but far smaller than the hour-of-day swing; weekends are reduced activity,
not building shutdown.

## 3. Peak demand

Example (building 1, `Peacock_education_Anita`): peak reading 32.7833 kWh
on 2016-05-28 14:00 local. Monthly peaks (`get_building_peaks`) show no
single dominant season for this building -- its highest calendar-month peak
(32.78 kWh, May) and its lowest (21.25 kWh, July) don't follow a simple
summer-cooling-load pattern, again consistent with weak weather sensitivity.
`06_peak_demand_distribution.png` shows per-building peak values spread
widely (tens to low thousands of kWh) because building size varies enormously
in this subset (see section 4) -- a peak-kWh histogram compares buildings of very
different scale, so read it as "shape of the portfolio," not as a
like-for-like comparison.

## 4. Building comparison and candidate relationships

`05_building_comparison.png` ranks the top 20 of 60 buildings by mean hourly
kWh. Mean consumption spans **0.39 to 1,442.6 kWh** across the portfolio --
a ~3,700x range, driven overwhelmingly by building size and use, not noise.

Exploratory Pearson correlations against mean kWh (correlation, not
causation; each with its real sample size):

| Candidate feature | r | n | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `area_sqm` | **0.85** | 60 | Strong, and the full subset has this field -- the single most useful static feature found |
| `occupants` | 0.58 | 26 | Meaningful but only 26/60 buildings have this field populated |
| `number_of_floors` | 0.47 | 30 | Weaker, and only half the buildings have it |
| `year_built` | 0.06 | 8 | Not meaningful -- only 8/60 buildings have a year_built value, far too few to draw a conclusion |
| `eui`, `site_eui`, `source_eui`, `energy_star_rating` | -- | 0 | **Not computed.** 0 of 60 selected buildings have any of these fields populated in BDG2's metadata. This is not a bug or an oversight -- the source data simply doesn't have it for this subset, and no substitute value was invented for it. |

Mean-of-means kWh by `primary_use` (7 categories present): Education is
highest (~425 kWh), then Entertainment/public assembly (~280), Office and
Lodging/residential both ~87, Public services ~67, Other ~25, Warehouse ~14
(n=1, a single building). The ordering tracks typical building size in this
subset more than it isolates a use-type effect -- `area_sqm` and
`primary_use` are confounded here, not independently tested.

## 5. Energy intensity (kWh/m²)

Computed as `total_kwh / area_sqm` over the full 2-year window (`services/stats.py::energy_intensity`,
kept explicitly distinct from -- never substituted for -- the source EUI
fields, which are entirely unpopulated per section 4). Across the 60 buildings:
minimum 73.9 kWh/m², median 266.5 kWh/m², maximum 1,445.7 kWh/m² (2-year
total; roughly 37-723 kWh/m²/year). A 20x spread even after normalizing by
area -- size alone doesn't fully explain the variation; use-type and
equipment intensity clearly matter too.

## 6. Weather relationship

Pearson correlation of each site's total hourly consumption (summed across
its buildings) against weather variables, computed independently per site:

| Variable | Site 1 (Moose) r (n) | Site 2 (Gator) r (n) | Site 3 (Peacock) r (n) |
|---|---|---|---|
| air_temp_c | -0.001 (16,856) | 0.092 (17,103) | 0.084 (17,522) |
| dew_temp_c | -0.056 (16,810) | 0.118 (17,103) | -0.146 (17,522) |
| wind_speed | 0.168 (16,856) | -0.010 (17,106) | **0.301** (17,471) |
| cloud_coverage | undefined (0 pairs) | 0.088 (9,750) | 0.004 (11,626) |
| precip_1hr_mm | -0.025 (1,687) | 0.013 (17,097) | 0.011 (17,511) |

**Every correlation is weak** (|r| ≤ 0.30). This is consistent across all
three sites and corroborates the flat-looking temperature scatter in
`07_temperature_vs_energy.png` (Site 1: essentially no visible relationship
between outdoor temperature and total consumption). Two important caveats:
Pearson's r only captures *linear* relationships -- a real heating/cooling
U-shaped curve (higher consumption at both temperature extremes) would show
up as a weak linear correlation even if physically present, so this doesn't
rule out non-linear temperature sensitivity. Second, these are *site-level*
aggregates across mixed building types (Education, Office, Lodging, ...); a
real per-building or per-use-type weather sensitivity could be present and
cancel out in the site-level sum. Neither caveat was tested further in this
phase -- flagged as a candidate feature-engineering direction for Phase 7,
not a finding.

`cloud_coverage` at Site 1 has **zero** usable pairs -- every reading is
NULL for that site specifically (site-level weather completeness varies,
not just a uniform dataset-wide rate).

## 7. Missing-data findings

- **Duplicate observations: 0** in both `energy_measurements` and
  `weather_measurements` (structurally impossible given the composite
  primary keys, verified anyway).
- **Missing timestamps**: exactly 240 across all 60 sensors (4 each) --
  fully attributed to DST handling, documented in `docs/data_quality_notes.md`.
- **NULL consumption values are not uniform across sites**
  (`08_missing_data_overview.png`): every Gator-site building sits between
  ~2.9% and ~5.0% missing, while nearly every Moose and Peacock building is
  under 1%. This is a real, site-clustered pattern -- not random noise --
  and worth carrying into Phase 7: a per-site (or per-building) missingness
  feature, or at minimum awareness that Gator buildings will need more
  imputation, is warranted.
- **Weather completeness is very uneven by column**: `air_temp_c` (0.03%
  missing), `dew_temp_c`/`wind_speed` (~0.1%) and `sea_lvl_pressure` (1.3%)
  are effectively complete; `wind_direction` (3.9%) is usable;
  `precip_1hr_mm` (29.3%) and especially `cloud_coverage` (58.4%) and
  `precip_6hr_mm` (92.1%) are too sparse to be reliable model inputs without
  substantial imputation or exclusion.
- **Negative values: 0** (nulled at ingestion time, per Phase 4; reconfirmed
  here by construction).

## 8. What this means for forecasting (Phase 7 preparation)

**Target variable**: `energy_measurements.consumption_kwh`, per building
(one electricity sensor per building in this subset).

**Time resolution**: hourly, matching the source data and the stated
day-ahead (24-hour) forecasting goal.

**Available historical context**: up to 17,544 hourly points per building
(2016-01-01 to 2017-12-31), 17,501 with a non-null reading for the example
building above -- typically ~95-99% complete depending on site (see section 7).

**Candidate features, ranked by what this phase actually found**:
1. Calendar/time features (hour-of-day, day-of-week local time) -- by far
   the strongest observed signal (section 2): a 1.33x peak/trough swing and a
   14-17% weekday premium, both cleanly measured.
2. `area_sqm` -- strong (r=0.85, n=60, fully populated) static feature for
   scaling predictions across buildings.
3. Lagged consumption (not tested this phase, but the standard baseline
   feature for a target this autocorrelated at hourly resolution).
4. Weather variables -- weak linear signal in this data (section 6); include as
   candidates (especially wind_speed at Site 3) but do not expect them to
   dominate, and consider non-linear transforms (e.g. heating/cooling
   degree hours) rather than raw temperature.
5. `primary_use` -- plausible categorical feature, but confounded with
   `area_sqm` in this subset (section 4); worth testing but expect it to add less
   once area is already included.
6. `occupants` / `number_of_floors` -- only 43%/50% populated; usable but
   need a missing-value strategy (see below), not silently drop-and-forget.
7. `year_built`, `eui`/`site_eui`/`source_eui`/`energy_star_rating` -- **not
   viable features** for this subset (8/60 and 0/60 populated respectively).

**Leakage risks identified**:
- Any feature computed with a centered or backward-looking-but-including-
  the-target-hour window (e.g. a same-day daily total used to predict one of
  its own constituent hours) would leak.
- Weather "actuals" (what this dataset provides) are not the same as a
  weather *forecast* a real day-ahead system would have at prediction time;
  training on actuals and treating them as if they were forecasts available
  24h in advance would overstate real-world accuracy unless explicitly
  caveated.
- A random train/test split would leak future information into training
  (autocorrelated hourly data) -- **the split must be chronological**.
- `predictions.generated_at` vs `predictions.target_ts` (already enforced in
  the Phase 2 schema) is the mechanism to make a leakage check assertable in
  code once Phase 7 starts writing predictions, not just a documentation
  promise.

**Missing-data considerations**: consumption NULLs cluster by site (Gator:
2.9-5.0%, others <1%) -- an imputation or masking strategy is needed before
these rows can be used as either features or targets, and it should account
for the fact that missingness is not MCAR (missing completely at random)
given the clear site clustering.

**Weather availability limitations**: weather is per-*site*, not
per-building (multiple buildings at a site share one weather reading) --
already reflected in the schema (`weather_measurements.site_id`, not
`building_id`). `cloud_coverage` and `precip_6hr_mm` are too sparse
dataset-wide to rely on; `air_temp_c`, `dew_temp_c`, `wind_speed` are safe
to use.

**Candidate train/validation/test strategy** (to be finalized in Phase 7,
not decided here): chronological split respecting the two-year window --
e.g. train on 2016, validate on early/mid-2017, test on the final months of
2017, or a rolling-origin (walk-forward) scheme for the day-ahead framing.
Whichever is chosen, it must never let a validation/test-period timestamp
influence a training-period feature.
